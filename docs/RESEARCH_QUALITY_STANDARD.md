# Research Quality Standard

## Acceptance principle

Synthetic qualification asks whether NORA identified plausible issues,
noticed material jurisdiction uncertainty, preserved important and adverse
facts, distinguished assertions from proof, formulated professional research
questions, searched multiple query families, checked hierarchy and contrary
authority, avoided unsupported conclusions, and exposed uncertainty.  It does
not ask only whether a preselected doctrinal label was guessed.

## Required gates

The following gates are tracked independently:

- `LAY_NARRATIVE_INGESTION`
- `JURISDICTION_RESOLUTION`
- `ISSUE_HYPOTHESIS_GENERATION`
- `QUERY_PLAN_COMPILATION`
- `PRIVATE_TO_PUBLIC_QUERY_ABSTRACTION`
- `LAY_LANGUAGE_RETRIEVAL_RECALL`
- `MULTI_PASS_RESEARCH_ORCHESTRATION`
- `PRO_SE_EXPLANATION_FIDELITY`

Fixture evidence may qualify a fixture gate.  A real-source gate cannot be
marked `PASS` from fixtures, mocks, or a provider envelope alone.

## Evidence standard

Every issue must retain its trigger facts, rationale, ontology source/version,
needed context, and unresolved questions.  Every query must retain its class,
purpose, constraints, variants, capabilities, and retrieval mode.  A provider
failure or empty result is never a no-law conclusion.

## Shared evidence

Lay and professional modes use the same source-faithful mirror, derived
retrieval records, provider contract, authority identity, opinion voice,
currentness, treatment, quote verification, adverse-authority preservation, and
human review requirements.  Simplification is an interaction concern, not a
lower research standard.
